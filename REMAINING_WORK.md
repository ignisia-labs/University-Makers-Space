## 1. Blocking a real deployment

These must be settled before students use the system.

### Everything runs on one laptop

Postgres and Redis are local Docker containers. Both must move to managed
services before deployment — see `DEPLOYMENT.md`.

### Nothing schedules the maintenance job

The API no longer runs background timers, so it deploys to a serverless platform
unchanged. The work still has to happen: `POST /api/v1/jobs/run` releases
no-show slots, closes overstayed visits and sends queued email, and something
must call it every minute.

Until a scheduler is pointed at it, a team that never turns up keeps its slot
forever and no invitation email is ever sent. `DEPLOYMENT.md` §3 covers Render
Cron, Vercel Cron and a plain external scheduler.

## 2. Known gaps in what is built

### The catalogue images are drawings, not photographs
Every machine and domain has artwork, generated by
`scripts/apply-catalogue-art.ts` and hosted on Cloudinary. They are studio-style
product renders drawn in SVG — one per category, not per machine.

They are not photographs, and could not be. The free keyword photo services
return loosely-matched images with attribution watermarked into the pixels; a
request for "oscilloscope" came back with a 1970s museum equipment rack. That
looks worse on a card than an honest illustration.

Replace any of them by uploading a real photo through **Administration →
Equipment**; the upload path is verified working.

### Most of the equipment does not exist
`scripts/seed-demo-catalogue.ts` created 29 machines across the five domains so
the catalogue, kit picker and admin screens can be demonstrated for every
branch. Only the 24 machines imported from the JN007/JN110/JN305 sheets are
real.

Every fabricated row's description ends with
`[demo item — not part of the audited inventory]`, and

```
npx tsx scripts/seed-demo-catalogue.ts --remove --commit
```

deletes exactly those rows. **Do this before students use the system**, or they
will plan projects around a laser cutter the MakerSpace does not own — which is
the same failure the old seed caused with a single 3D printer.

### The email sender address is the Brevo login
`MAIL_FROM` is `MIT-WPU MakerSpace <b69efa001@smtp-brevo.com>`. Mail is being
delivered, but students see a machine-generated Brevo address as the sender.
Verify a proper sender in Brevo — ideally `no-reply@mitwpu.edu.in`, which needs
the domain verified — and change `MAIL_FROM` to it. Deliverability improves too;
mail from an unrelated domain is more likely to be filtered.

`sandeep.singhal@mitwu.edu.in` is missing the *p* in `mitwpu`. He was skipped.

### Consumable units are guessed
The import read units from the quantity cell (`1 Bundle` → bundle). Most rows
had a bare number and defaulted to "piece". Check anything measured in grams or
metres.

## 3. Not built at all

### Phase 7 remainder
- **Notification preferences.** The settings screen lists what the app notifies
  you about and states plainly that choosing between them is not available. The
  backend has no per-user preference model.
- **Real-time updates.** Availability changes are published to a Redis channel
  but nothing subscribes. Screens poll on a timer (20–30s) instead. A booking
  taken by another team appears on the next poll, not instantly.

### Phase 8 — entirely
- CI/CD pipeline
- Monitoring, alerting and error tracking
- Load testing against the 500-concurrent-user target. The booking and claim
  paths are built for it — row locks, conditional updates, idempotency keys —
  and their concurrency suites pass, but no one has driven 500 real sessions
  through it.
- Database backups and a restore drill
- A staging environment

### Testing
- The 12 suites under `backend/tests/` are named "integration" but mock Prisma.
  The seven suites in `backend/scripts/verify-*.ts` are the ones that exercise a
  real database; those are the ones to trust.
- No frontend tests at all — no component, no end-to-end. Every UI claim in this
  project's history was verified by hand in a browser.
- No accessibility audit.

### Product gaps
- **Events** have a backend module and routes but no interface.
- **Reports** export CSV; nothing charts or schedules them.
- **A student cannot leave a project** — only the leader can remove members.
- **No password-less recovery** if a student loses access to their Google
  account; an administrator must unlink the identity by hand.
